# Naphtha-to-chemicals — the downstream pivot (technical backbone)

An independent authority underpins the whole "petrol → petrochemicals" thesis in this repo:
**Dr. Marcio Wagner da Silva** (crude-oil refining process engineer/manager, PhD Chemical Eng.,
UNICAMP, Brazil) has a large LinkedIn/technical series arguing exactly this pivot. His work supplies
the process detail behind the import-substitution economics here.

## The strategic frame — gasoline is a "Red Ocean"

Da Silva's framing: gasoline is a **"red ocean"** — commoditised, low-differentiation, low-profitability,
and structurally **declining** (EV adoption, fuel-efficiency gains, regulation, lighter crudes). Against
it, **petrochemicals are the "blue ocean"**: growing ~**4.25%/yr in Asia**, with Asia projected to be
**~90% of world crude-demand growth (2019-2026), driven by petrochemicals**, not fuel.

This is the same force this repo models from the demand side — **ethanol + EVs capping India's petrol
demand and freeing petrol-range feedstock** — seen from the refiner's side: the fuel pool is the red
ocean; the freed naphtha/reformate should be steered to chemicals.

## The two halves of the conversion

**① Olefins half** — steam cracker / FCC → ethylene & propylene:
| Propylene route | Type | Yield / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Steam cracking** (naphtha) | by-product of ethylene | ~15% propylene |
| **FCC** (refinery) | by-product, upgradable | ~30% of the LPG cut; propylene worth ~**2.5× LPG** |
| **PDH** (propane dehydrogenation) | **on-purpose** | Pt/alumina, ~600 °C |
| **Metathesis** (ethylene + butene → propylene) | on-purpose | economics = C3–C2 price gap |
| **MTO** (methanol-to-olefins) | gas-based | high hydrocarbon selectivity |

→ derivatives: **polypropylene, acrylonitrile, propylene oxide, cumene, acrylic acid** — i.e. India's
propylene-chain imports (PP ₹15,627 cr, ACN ₹2,283 cr, phenol via cumene, polyols via PO, SAP via
acrylic acid, DCPC FY24-25).

**② Aromatics half** — catalytic reformer + aromatics complex → BTX (see `naphtha_btx_routes.md`):
PAREX/ISOMAR → paraxylene → PTA/PET (₹24,538 cr, the biggest cluster); benzene → styrene (₹11,105 cr),
polycarbonate (₹5,545 cr), MDI; TATORAY upgrades surplus toluene (₹4,669 cr) to benzene + xylenes.

Da Silva's key synergy point: **steam-cracking (olefins) and the aromatics complex share feed and
utilities** — integrating both extracts maximum value per barrel.

## Economics — the recurring number

Across his articles, **integrated refiners add ~$0.68–2.02/bbl** of crude vs fuels-focused peers —
the same band as the Digital Refining figure used here (+$1.5–2/bbl marginal GRM), and well below the
full crude-to-chemicals step-change ($60–80/bbl vs $15–25). FCC propylene at ~2.5× LPG value is a
**low-capex, on-purpose** upgrade already sitting inside India's existing refineries.

## The India read
- India's **FCC units already co-produce propylene** — recovering/upgrading it (or bolting on PDH) is a
  low-capex path to substitute **PP imports (₹15,627 cr)** — the single largest petrochemical import.
- The **aromatics half** (reformer → PX/benzene) targets the biggest cluster — **polyester (PTA/PX) and
  styrenics** — with the reformer that today makes petrol octane.
- The pivot is not speculative engineering: it is the documented direction of the global downstream
  industry, and India's ethanol/EV-freed petrol pool is precisely the "red ocean" feedstock to redeploy.
