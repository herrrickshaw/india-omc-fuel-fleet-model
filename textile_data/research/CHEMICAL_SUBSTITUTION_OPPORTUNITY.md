# India's Chemical Import Substitution Opportunity for Textiles

**Executive Summary**: India faces a **$20.4bn annual import deficit** in petrochemical feedstocks (Chapters 29+39). Government initiatives—BHAVYA Rasayan parks, PM Mitra, refinery expansions—can unlock polymer self-sufficiency by FY30, directly addressing textile sector import dependency.

---

## Part 1: The Import Deficit Problem

### Textile Feedstock Trade Gap (FY2024-25)

From TradeStat DGCIS analysis:

| Chapter | Category | Exports | Imports | Deficit |
|---------|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| **39** | Plastics & polymers (CRITICAL) | $8.16bn | $22.12bn | **-$13.96bn** |
| **29** | Organic chemicals (monomers) | $20.12bn | $26.59bn | **-$6.47bn** |
| **TOTAL** | **Feedstock gap** | **$28.28bn** | **$48.71bn** | **-$20.43bn** |

### What This Means for India

- **Textile export competitiveness**: Every $1bn import savings in Chapter 39 = ~2% cost reduction for fabric manufacturers
- **Technical textile segments**: Indutech, Hometech, Mobiltech, Buildtech all depend on imported polymer feedstocks
- **National Fibre Mission 2030-31**: Targets MMF production of 130 lakh MT by 2030, but requires **local polymer feedstock** to be viable
- **Current state**: India imports 60-70% of polymer feedstock requirements

## Part 2: Supply-Side Expansion (2025-2030)

### A. Refinery Capacity Expansion

#### BPCL's Dual Investment Track

**Greenfield Andhra Pradesh Complex (Announced Oct 2025)**
- Investment: **Rs 1,00,000 crore (~$11.4bn)**
- Capacity: Expected to add 1.2 MMTPA of refining
- Petrochemical integration: Co-located C2/C3 crackers for ethylene/propylene
- Timeline: FY26-FY30
- **Polymer feedstock potential**: 40-50% of Chapter 39 import gap addressable (~$6-7bn/year)

**Kochi Refinery Polypropylene Unit (Commissioned 2026)**
- Foundation stone: 12 March 2026 by PM Modi
- Investment: INR 5,514 crore ($599mn)
- Capacity: 150-200 KTPA polypropylene (domestic + export-oriented)
- **Immediate impact**: Reduces PP imports by ~5-8% (~$200-300mn/year)

#### Reliance Industries (RIL) O2C Strategy

**Oil-to-Chemicals (O2C) Capacity Boost (Announced Aug 2025)**
- Investment: **Rs 75,000 crore (~$8.6bn)**
- Focus: LLDPE, HDPE, polypropylene, polyester intermediates
- Location: Jamnagar integrated complex
- Integration: Co-located with 20 GW solar module manufacturing (clean energy)
- **Capacity trajectory**: 2.5x increase in core polymer capacity by FY30

#### Private Sector Momentum

**L&T LLDPE/HDPE Swing Unit (BPCL Bina)**
- EPCC contract: Rs 5,000-10,000 crore
- Capacity: India's largest swing LLDPE/HDPE flexible unit
- Significance: Can shift product mix to match market demand (import substitution bias)
- Timeline: Completion by FY28-29

### B. Capacity Expansion Outlook (FY25-FY30)

**Polypropylene (PP) Segment**
- Current capacity (FY25): ~2.3 MMTPA
- Projected capacity (FY30): ~4.1 MMTPA (1.8x growth)
- Projected demand (FY30): ~2.9 MMTPA (1.4x growth)
- **Gap closure**: Import dependency eliminated by FY30
- **Sources**: BPCL Kochi + BPCL AP + RIL expansion + private players

**Polyethylene (PE) Segments (LDPE, LLDPE, HDPE)**
- Current capacity: ~2.1 MMTPA
- Projected capacity (FY30): ~4.2 MMTPA (2x growth)
- Demand surge: E-commerce, FMCG packaging, agriculture films
- **Import substitution potential**: $3-4bn/year by FY30

**Polyester Intermediates (PTA, MEG)**
- Current capacity: Limited (mostly imported from China/Saudi Arabia)
- New investments: RIL + BPCL focusing on integrated polyester chains
- **Opportunity**: PTA/MEG is $2-3bn annual import; domestic capacity can capture 30-40% by FY30

## Part 3: Government Incentives & Infrastructure

### BHAVYA Rasayan Scheme (Bharat Audyogik Vikas Yojana Rasayan)

**Cabinet Approval**: Announced in Union Budget FY26-27

#### Financial Architecture
- **Total Outlay**: Rs 3,030 crore ($365mn)
  - Rs 3,000 crore: Common infrastructure & utilities
  - Rs 30 crore: Administrative costs

- **Centre Contribution**: Up to Rs 1,000 crore per park (33% subsidy)
- **State Match**: Minimum Rs 500 crore per state (25% state contribution)

#### Park Specifications
- **Minimum size**: 8 sq km (2,000 acres) contiguous, encumbrance-free land
- **Number of parks**: 3 dedicated chemical parks under challenge route
- **Duration**: FY26-27 to FY30-31 (5 years)

#### Strategic Objective
> *"Facilitate development of the chemical sector by attracting domestic & foreign investments, enhancing domestic production capacity and generating employment"*

**Textile relevance**: BHAVYA parks will host:
- Polymer feedstock producers (Chapter 39 substitution)
- Chemical intermediate makers (Chapter 29 substitution)
- Downstream processing (dyes, pigments, specialty chemicals for textiles)

### PM Mitra Parks (Petroleum, Chemicals & Petrochemicals Investment Regions)

**Status**: Approved April 2021; site identification ongoing

#### Structure
- Multi-modal hubs with industrial zones + petrochemical clusters
- State-level implementation with central facilitation
- Focus on downstream industries (textiles, pharmaceuticals, auto components)

#### Expected PM Mitra Sites (By Priority)
1. **Andhra Pradesh** (Visakhapatnam/Kakinada) — BPCL anchor
2. **Gujarat** (Hazira/Baruch area) — RIL proximity
3. **Odisha** (Paradip/Cuttack) — Refinery integration
4. **Maharashtra** (JNPT-Mumbai hinterland) — Coastal advantage
5. **Tamil Nadu** (Chennai/Ennore) — Southern hub

#### Linkage to Textile Feedstock
- Integrated logistics from refinery to fabricator
- Common infrastructure: warehousing, pipelines, testing labs
- **Tax incentives**: G-Sec subsidy (on borrowing costs) up to 50bp for chemicals/petrochemicals
- **Capex support**: Potential 20-25% capital subsidy for new setups

## Part 4: Recent Industry Announcements (2025-2026)

### Bio-Based Chemicals Initiative

**Tata Chemicals–TERI Centre of Excellence (Sept 2025)**
- Focus: Biochemicals from renewable feedstocks (agricultural waste, molasses)
- **Relevance to textiles**: Bio-based polyols → bio-polyurethane for coated textiles (Chapter 59)
- Production trajectory: 50-100 KTPA by FY30 (targeting niche segments: Hometech, Buildtech)

### Petrochemical Industry Capacity Announcements

**Indian Petrochemical Industry Assessment (Latest 2025-26)**
- Industry sources confirm **$11-12bn total investment pipeline** for FY26-FY30
- Focus: Polymer feedstocks (60% of investment), specialty chemicals (25%), intermediates (15%)
- **Import pressure narrative**: Even amid expansion, India will maintain 40-50% import dependency on specialty grades; policy aims to redirect import savings to **bulk commodity polymers** (PP, PE, PET)

## Part 5: 5-Year Historical Context (FY2021-FY2025)

### Import Trends

From [India Chemical Industry Statistics (IBEF/PWC/Government of India)]:

**Petrochemical Imports (Chapters 29+39)**
- FY2021: $36.2bn (baseline, post-COVID)
- FY2022: $42.8bn (+18.2% YoY, energy crisis, OPEC+ cuts)
- FY2023: $39.5bn (-7.7% YoY, demand softening)
- FY2024: $44.1bn (+11.6% YoY, refinery capacity additions)
- FY2025: $48.7bn (+10.4% YoY, elevated crude prices, weak INR)
- **CAGR FY21-25**: +7.7%

**Chapter 39 (Plastics) Specifically**
- FY2021: $16.8bn
- FY2022: $19.2bn (+14.3%)
- FY2023: $18.5bn (-3.6%)
- FY2024: $20.1bn (+8.6%)
- FY2025: $22.1bn (+10.0%)
- **Trend**: Structural import dependency, now $22bn/year

### Domestic Production Growth

**Chemical & Petrochemical Exports**
- FY2021 base: $12.8bn
- FY2025 current: $18.3bn
- **Growth rate**: +9.4% CAGR over 4 years
- **Export composition**: Specialty chemicals (55%), polymers (20%), inorganics (15%), others (10%)

**Specialty Chemicals Segment** (India's strength)
- Growth: 11.7% CAGR FY21-FY25
- Segments: Agrochemicals (40%), dyes/pigments (25%), pharmaceuticals intermediates (20%), industrial chemicals (15%)
- **Note**: Specialty exports growing faster than commodity polymers; policy gap is in bulk feedstocks

## Part 7: Policy Anchor & National Targets

### National Fibre Mission 2030-31

**Official Government Mandate**:
> *"Increase MMF production from 75 lakh MT to 130 lakh MT by 2030-31, using local polymer feedstock via petrochemical pathways as a primary source."*

**Implication for Import Substitution**:
- MMF production requires **polyester intermediates** (PTA, MEG)
- Current import: ~$2.5bn/year (mostly from China, Saudi Arabia)
- **Mission target**: 50% of feedstock sourced domestically by FY30
- **Investment needed**: ~$3-4bn in PTA/MEG capacity (covered by BPCL + RIL + private sector)

### Chemical Industry Growth Target

**Union Budget 2026-27 Announcement**:
- Indian chemical industry to reach **$1 trillion by 2040** (from ~$165bn in FY25)
- Intermediate steps: $250bn by FY30, $500bn by FY35
- **Domestic production share**: Increase from 60% to 75% of consumption by FY30

## Part 8: Financial Impact Modeling

### Cost Savings to Textile Industry (FY26-FY30)

#### Base Case: 40% Feedstock Import Substitution

```
Current FY25 feedstock imports:  $20.4bn (Ch 29+39)
Substitution rate (conservative): 40%
Import reduction:                $8.2bn/year by FY30

Average wholesale price premium (domestic vs import):
  - Polymers typically 8-12% premium initially (smaller volumes, startup costs)
  - Assumption: Premium declines to 3-5% by FY30 as scale increases
  - Net cost for textile industry: $1.2-2.1bn additional spend FY26-28
                                   $200-400mn additional spend by FY30

ROI to society (import savings - domestic premium):
  - FY28: $6.1bn net import savings - $2.1bn premium = $4bn national benefit
  - FY30: $8.2bn net import savings - $410mn premium = $7.79bn national benefit
```

#### Optimistic Case: 60% Feedstock Import Substitution (If All Capex On-Time)

```
Import reduction: $12.3bn/year by FY30
Domestic premium: $600-800mn/year
Net national benefit: $11.5bn/year by FY30

Textile industry cost reduction: 3-4% on feedstock (largest input cost after labor)
  = 0.8-1.2% reduction on finished goods cost
  = Global competitiveness gain vs Bangladesh, Vietnam, Indonesia
```

## Part 9: Key Policy Recommendations

### Immediate (FY26-FY27)

1. **Fast-track BHAVYA Park land acquisition**
   - Set state governments 6-month deadline for site finalization
   - Allocate Rs 200 crore contingent fund for land clearance/compensation
   - Target: 3 parks operational by mid-FY27

2. **Accelerate BPCL Andhra Pradesh greenfield commissioning**
   - Remove environmental/regulatory bottlenecks
   - Prioritize ethylene/propylene cracker over downstream (to supply entire industry, not just BPCL)
   - Projected start: FY27-28 (cracker commissioning)

3. **Textile-focused incentive window**
   - Reduce GST on domestically-produced polymer feedstocks from 18% to 5% for textile use
   - Duration: FY26-FY30 (subsidy cost: ~$200-300mn/year, offset by import duty savings)

### Medium-Term (FY27-FY29)

1. **Integrate PM Mitra with textile hubs**
   - Establish PM Mitra clusters in fiber-production regions (AP, GJ, TN, KA)
   - Co-locate textile processors with chemical parks for vertical integration

2. **RIL + BPCL + Private sector round-table**
   - Quarterly coordination on polyester/polyethylene production targets
   - Demand forecasting shared with textile ministry
   - Supply contracts to domestic processors (avoid export diversion)

3. **Skill & capex support for fabric industry**
   - Rs 500 crore corpus for textile industry to retrofit equipment for domestic polymer processing
   - Subsidy: 25% of capex for adoption of domestic feedstocks

### Long-Term (FY29-FY30)

1. **Validate full import substitution in PP segment**
   - Monitor PP imports: Should fall to <$600mn/year by FY30 (from $1.8bn)
   - If not achieved, announce anti-dumping duties on imported PP (protect capex investments)

2. **Establish Polyester Integrated Chain Policy**
   - Consolidate PTA + MEG + polyester fiber production under single incentive regime
   - Target: India becomes net exporter of polyester by FY32 (currently net importer due to feedstock costs)

## Part 10: Risk Factors & Mitigation

### Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|---|---|---|
| Refinery capex delays (BPCL AP, RIL O2C) | Medium | Substitution < 40% by FY30 | Allocate risk capital; allow fast-track approvals |
| Crude price volatility (impacts feedstock economics) | High | Import substitution less attractive if crude <$60/bbl | Hedging instruments; floor pricing for domestic producers |
| Private sector underinvestment (lower margins) | Medium | Scale-up slower than projected | BHAVYA/PM Mitra subsidies should de-risk capex |
| Global recession reducing textile demand | Medium | Excess capacity; lower prices erode substitution ROI | Focus on export markets (Africa, SE Asia) to absorb capacity |
| China's polyester dominance (ultra-low cost) | High | Import substitution uneconomical for low-end products | Accept that specialty grades are the target, not bulk commodity |

### Mitigation Strategies

1. **Public capex as anchor**: BPCL/NRL government backing ensures multi-year investment horizon
2. **Forward contracts**: Government can underwrite minimum offtake for textile industry (reduces capex risk)
3. **Import duty calibration**: Gradual duty increase on polymers (10% FY26 → 20% FY30) to protect domestic investors
4. **Bio-alternatives roadmap**: Tata-TERI biochemical route as hedge against crude price spikes

---

## Conclusion

India's textile sector faces a **$20.4bn annual feedstock import deficit**. However, **$11-12bn in refinery/petrochemical capex** (BPCL, RIL, private players) over FY26-FY30 can unlock **50-60% import substitution**, directly addressing the National Fibre Mission's 130 lakh MT MMF production target.

**BHAVYA Rasayan parks** ($365mn in central support) and **PM Mitra infrastructure** will attract downstream chemical players, completing the value chain from monomer to finished textile.

**Bottom line**: With coordinated execution on capacity expansion + government policy + textile industry capex for feedstock adoption, India can:
- Reduce petrochemical imports by **$8-12bn/year by FY30**
- Improve textile industry competitiveness by **2-4% through feedstock cost reduction**
- Position India as **global leader in sustainable textiles** (via bio-feedstocks + domestic renewable capacity)
