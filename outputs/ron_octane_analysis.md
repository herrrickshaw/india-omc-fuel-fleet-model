# The RON95 opportunity — ethanol's octane credit, and who pockets it

Ethanol is energy-poor (−34%/L) but octane-rich: blending RON ~112 against regular petrol's 91 (IS 2796). Every blend step carries an octane credit. Today India blends to a **final RON 91**, which means the credit is taken upstream — refiners back the blendstock (BOB) off to cheaper, lower-octane material — rather than delivered to the vehicle as a higher-RON fuel.

## 1. Two RON accounting routes per blend

| Blend | (a) BOB refiners need if pump stays RON 91 | (b) Pump RON if BOB stays 91 | Octane credit (₹/L at pump-premium value) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E10 | 88.7 | 93.1 | 4.73 |
| E20 | 85.7 | 95.2 | 9.45 |
| E25 | 84.0 | 96.2 | 11.81 |
| E27 | 83.2 | 96.7 | 12.76 |
| E30 | 82.0 | 97.3 | 14.17 |

- Route (a) — today: at E20 the refinery only has to make **~85.7-RON blendstock** (5 points below the old spec). Lower reforming severity, more cheap naphtha in the pool — a refining-cost saving retained in the supply chain, on top of the tax headroom the parity analysis found.
- Route (b) — the opportunity: the same E20 on an unchanged 91-RON BOB is a **RON 95.2 fuel** — premium-grade octane, nationally, at zero extra refining cost. E25→E30 walks the pump to RON 96.3–97.3.
- Priced at the XP95 pump spread (~₹9/L for 4 RON points), the ethanol octane credit at E20 is worth **~₹9.4/L** — value the consumer neither sees on the price board nor gets in the tank.

## 2. RON95 closes the mileage gap the energy math opened

A RON95-labelled E20 lets OEMs raise compression (CR ~10.5 → 12, ~1.5 points): at ~1.5%/CR point that is **+2.2% efficiency**, against E20's −4% energy drop → net mileage ≈ **-1.8% vs E0** on an E20-RON95-optimised engine.

- This is Brazil's actual playbook (E27 on ~RON 95+ regular, high-CR engines) and why its fleet does not experience the headline mileage penalty.
- It composes with the Volume Dividend scenarios: **parity pricing makes the consumer whole *today* (existing fleet); RON95 labelling + E20+ engines make the penalty physically disappear over fleet turnover** (~7% of the parc turns per year — Vahan CY2025 2.93 cr registrations).
- The octane credit also strengthens S1: the ₹6.5–9.7/L embedded tax headroom understates total blend headroom by the ~₹9.4/L refining-side octane saving at E20 (₹14.2/L at E30).

## 3. Caveats

- Blending RON is non-linear and BOB-dependent; 112 is a central lever (literature 108–115). Route-(a) BOB numbers are linear-blend estimates.
- The ₹/RON value uses the retail XP95 spread as the willingness-to-pay signal; actual refining cost per RON point is lower (reforming severity ~₹0.3–0.8/L per 4-5 points) — both are levers in the CSV.
- Higher CR needs the E20+/FFV-ready fleet (Vahan tracks PETROL(E20) as its own fuel type — 21% of CY2025 registrations); the gain arrives with turnover, not by decree.
- CBG needs no octane accounting: methane's RON-equivalent is ~120+ and CNG engines already run CR 12+ — another reason the gas side has no hidden ledger.
