# Price-adjustment scenarios: neutralising the blend mileage loss

If blended petrol delivered honest **cost-per-km**, its pump price would be discounted by exactly the mileage it takes away: `P_blend = P_E0 x (1-drop)`. This note quantifies that discount for E20-E30 and tests four ways to fund it, using the ethanol procurement, grain-price and taxation numbers from the companion repos (vehicle_fuel_mileage, omc_model, statewise_tax_impact).

## 1. The parity discount

| Blend | Mileage drop | Parity discount (₹/L) | Parity pump price | National cost (₹ cr/yr) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E20 | 4.0% | 4.2 | 100.8 | 22,703 |
| E25 | 5.5% | 5.78 | 99.22 | 31,712 |
| E30 | 7.0% | 7.35 | 97.65 | 41,011 |

At ₹105/L, E20 should sell at ₹100.8/L for the consumer to be indifferent per km. The FY23 budget's ₹2/L penalty on UNblended petrol is a stick pointing the other way — it widens the gap instead of closing it.

## 2. Scenario S1 — pass through the tax break already embedded (preferred)

From the bang-for-your-buck tax analysis: the ethanol molecule escapes both central excise (₹19.9/L) and state VAT (~₹19.5/L), paying only 5% GST (~₹3.1/L on ₹62 ethanol). Today that saving is retained in the price build-up. Netting off ethanol's cost premium over refinery petrol (₹62 vs ₹58):

| Blend | Embedded headroom (₹/L) | Parity needs (₹/L) | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| E20 | 6.46 | 4.2 | 1.54× |
| E25 | 8.07 | 5.78 | 1.40× |
| E30 | 9.69 | 7.35 | 1.32× |

**The embedded tax break more than funds parity at every blend level.** Passing it through prices E20 honestly with ~₹2/L still left in the chain — no NEW subsidy, no NEW revenue loss vs an unblended counterfactual; it stops collecting a windfall the blend walk silently creates. (What it does end is the exchequer/OMC gain from the volume effect — that gain IS the consumer's loss.)

## 3. Scenarios S2-S4 — who else could fund it

| Blend | S2 excise cut → ₹/L left | S2 centre cost (₹ cr) | S3 VAT left (₹/L) | S4 ethanol price needed | S4 feasible vs grain floor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E20 | 15.7 | 22,703 | 15.3 | ₹41.0/L | NO |
| E25 | 14.12 | 31,712 | 13.72 | ₹38.9/L | NO |
| E30 | 12.55 | 41,011 | 12.15 | ₹37.5/L | NO |

**S4 (cheaper ethanol) fails on grain economics.** Parity funded from procurement alone needs ethanol at ₹41/L (E20) falling to ₹37/L (E30) — far below every ESY 2024-25 feedstock slab:

| Feedstock | OMC procurement (₹/L) |
|---|---|
| C-heavy molasses | 57.97 |
| FCI surplus rice | 58.50 |
| B-heavy molasses | 60.73 |
| Damaged food grains | 64.00 |
| Sugarcane juice/syrup | 65.61 |
| Maize | 71.86 |

Grain/cane mandi prices set these floors (maize at ₹71.86/L is the marginal slab the E30 walk leans on — the *most* expensive, moving procurement cost UP not down). Squeezing procurement to the cheapest slab funds only ₹0.81/L of E20's ₹4.2/L need (~19%). Ethanol economics cannot pay for parity; the tax side can.

## 4. Bottom line

- Honest pricing = ₹4.20/₹5.78/₹7.35 per litre off E20/E25/E30 at a ₹105 pump.
- The money already exists inside the price build-up: the dual-tax exemption on the ethanol fraction (S1) covers parity ~1.5× at every blend.
- Making the centre (S2) or states (S3) fund it via duty cuts costs ₹22,700-41,000 cr/yr — politically identical to today's arrangement in reverse, which is precisely why the discount hasn't happened.
- Grain prices make S4 impossible: parity-priced ethanol would have to be bought below its cheapest feedstock cost.
- CBG needs NO such scenario — energy parity per kg is physical, not fiscal.
