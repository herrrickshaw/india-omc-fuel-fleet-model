# The case for a state SGST on ethanol (1-5%), price-fixed, from E20 to E30

**Premise.** Ethanol (~₹60/L) is cheaper than the petrol base + central excise it displaces (~₹75/L), so at a **fixed pump price** every litre of ethanol opens ~₹15/L of fiscal space. Today states capture almost none of it — only the 2.5% SGST half of ethanol's 5% GST. A dedicated **state SGST on ethanol**, funded from that space, is new state revenue at zero cost to the consumer.

## 1. State SGST revenue — blend × rate grid (₹ crore/yr)

| Blend | Ethanol (bn L) | SGST 1% | 2% | 3% | 4% | 5% |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| E20 | 10.8 | 649 | 1,297 | 1,946 | 2,594 | 3,243 |
| E22 | 11.9 | 732 | 1,465 | 2,197 | 2,930 | 3,662 |
| E25 | 13.5 | 865 | 1,730 | 2,594 | 3,459 | 4,324 |
| E27 (Brazil) | 14.6 | 957 | 1,915 | 2,872 | 3,829 | 4,787 |
| E30 | 16.2 | 1,103 | 2,205 | 3,308 | 4,410 | 5,513 |

Revenue rises on **both** axes — higher rate and higher blend. Going E20→E30 lifts the 5% take from ₹3,243 cr to ₹5,513 cr; the ethanol base grows 50% as blend goes 20→30%.

## 2. The recovery insight — rate sets the %, blend sets the ₹

Because both the SGST take **and** the foregone petrol-VAT scale with ethanol volume, the *fraction* of foregone VAT a state recovers depends only on the **rate**:

> recovery ≈ (SGST rate × ethanol price) ÷ petrol-VAT-per-litre

| SGST rate | Recovery of foregone petrol-VAT |
|---|--:|
| 1% | 4% |
| 2% | 7% |
| 3% | 11% |
| 4% | 14% |
| 5% | 18% |

So 1-5% recovers ~3-18% of the foregone VAT at any blend. **Full recovery needs ~27%** — still price-neutral (it fits inside the ₹9/L space) but a heavy levy on green fuel. The realistic play is a modest rate on a big base.

## 3. Price stays fixed — the levy fits the fiscal space

| Blend | Ethanol price | Fiscal space ₹/L | 5% levy ₹/L | Price-neutral? |
|---|--:|--:|--:|:--:|
| E20 | ₹60.0 | ₹15.0 | ₹3.00 | ✅ |
| E22 | ₹61.6 | ₹13.4 | ₹3.08 | ✅ |
| E25 | ₹64.0 | ₹11.0 | ₹3.20 | ✅ |
| E27 (Brazil) | ₹65.6 | ₹9.4 | ₹3.28 | ✅ |
| E30 | ₹68.0 | ₹7.0 | ₹3.40 | ✅ |

A 5% ethanol SGST costs ₹3-3.4/L — comfortably inside the space at every blend, so the **consumer price never moves**. The space shrinks as ethanol gets pricier at high blends (grain/imported feedstock), which is what eventually bounds the design.

## 4. Is there a Brazil-E27 sweet spot?

State SGST revenue is monotone in blend and rate, so the optimum is a **constrained** one, set by the fleet, not a revenue peak. Brazil settled at **E27** as the practical ceiling for regular (non-flex) engines (now moving to E30 with a flex fleet). India's fleet is E20-rated in 2025, stretching toward E27 as E20+ vehicles diffuse.

**Sweet spot = E27 (Brazil) @ 5% SGST:** ~**₹4,787 cr/yr** of new, price-neutral state revenue (levy ₹3.28/L inside ₹9/L space), recovering ~18% of the foregone VAT — the highest blend India's non-flex fleet tolerates, mirroring Brazil. E30 @ 5% would add more (₹5,513 cr) but needs a flex/E20+ fleet and pricier ethanol, so it is the frontier, not the near-term optimum.

## 5. The case, in one line

Blending is mandated and rising anyway; ethanol is cheaper than the taxed petrol it replaces; so a **1-5% state SGST on ethanol, funded from the ₹15/L cost saving, hands states ₹0.6-4.8k cr/yr of new revenue with the pump price untouched** — and the pragmatic design point is **E27 (Brazil) at ~5%**, India's Brazil-style equilibrium, with room to push the rate toward ~30% if states ever want full VAT-parity recovery.

## 6. Caveats

- Recovery is *partial* at 1-5% (~3-18%); this augments, not replaces, the VAT base. It reallocates the ethanol fiscal space (largely foregone central excise) toward states — a centre↔state transfer question as much as a tax-design one.
- Ethanol price/blend supply curve, VAT-per-litre and the ₹15/L space are editable estimates. Fleet ceiling (E27) is an engineering/Brazil-analog assumption, not a mandate.
- Keeps GST-council reality aside: ethanol GST is centrally set; a *dedicated state* ethanol levy would need a GST-council/legal route. Modeled as an economic proposal.
