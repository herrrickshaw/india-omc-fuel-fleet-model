# Bio-isobutanol retrofit model — 100 KLPD grain distillery → 70 KLPD isobutanol

Retrofit capex ₹80 cr (engineered organism, decanter separation train — isobutanol is
NOT water-miscible, tankage, safety). 70:30 debt at 9.5%, **no subvention assumed**
(none exists for isobutanol). 15-yr life, 90% utilisation, year-1 ramp 55%.

## Why isobutanol at all — the two structural advantages
| Metric | Ethanol | Isobutanol | Advantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy, MJ/L | 21.1 | 26.5 | **+26% per litre** |
| Blend dilution at 20% | −6.9% | −3.5% | **half the mileage penalty** |
| ATJ/SAF yield, t distillate per t | 0.6 | 0.75 | **+25% more jet fuel** |
| Water miscibility / phase separation | yes (blending-terminal only) | no | **pipeline-transportable** |
| Vapour pressure blending effect | raises RVP | neutral/lowers | easier summer-grade compliance |

## Per-litre economics (maize route)
| Item | ₹/L |
|---|---|
| Realisation asked (policy gate) | 88.00 |
| Maize @ ₹23,000/t ÷ 280 L/t | (82.14) |
| DDGS credit 1.10 kg × ₹17 | 18.70 |
| Conversion (separation-heavy) | (18.00) |
| **Margin** | **6.56** |

Energy parity with the maize ethanol slab (₹71.86) would be **₹90.3/L**;
the pitch asks ₹88 — *below* parity, so the OMC buys isobutanol energy cheaper than
ethanol energy.

## Returns
| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EBITDA | ₹13.6 cr/yr |
| Project IRR | **13.8%** |
| Equity IRR | **16.9%** |
| NPV @ 12% | ₹8.1 cr |
| Payback | year 7 |
| DSCR (steady) | 1.17× |
| **No-policy case** (sold at the ethanol slab ₹71.86) | margin ₹-9.58/L → IRR -90.0% |

**The no-policy row is the whole pitch.** Isobutanol makes ~30% fewer litres per tonne of grain,
so selling it at ethanol's per-LITRE slab is a guaranteed loss. A per-ENERGY (or dedicated)
price slab is the gate: with it, the project clears comfortably; without it, nothing gets built.

## The SAF leg — where isobutanol's premium actually lives

| Output routed to ATJ/SAF | Blended realisation ₹/L | Margin ₹/L | EBITDA ₹ cr | Project IRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 88.0 | 6.56 | 13.6 | 13.8% |
| 30% | 96.1 | 14.66 | 30.5 | 33.9% |
| 50% | 101.5 | 20.06 | 41.7 | 45.6% |

At ₹115/L under an ATJ offtake, routing **30% of output to SAF lifts the project from 13.8% to 33.9%**; at 50% it is 45.6%. Blending alone is marginal — **the SAF optionality is the investment case**, and isobutanol's +25% ATJ yield over ethanol is why the molecule is worth the technology risk at all.


Caveats: bio-isobutanol at Indian commercial scale is UNPROVEN — global players (Gevo, Butamax)
have run retrofits, but no Indian plant operates today; yield (280 L/t) and conversion
cost (₹18/L) are the two most uncertain levers and both are technology-risk, not market-risk.
No BIS blending standard for isobutanol exists in India (ethanol has IS 15464; petrol IS 2796) —
a standards ask sits alongside the price ask. SAF economics are indicative (ICAO ATJ factors) and
depend on the CORSIA/Indian SAF mandate trajectory. DDGS from a C4 fermentation needs feed-safety
clearance. Retrofit capex ₹60–100 cr band.
